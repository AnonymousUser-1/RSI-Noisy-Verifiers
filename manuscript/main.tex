\aistatstitle{Matched Error Rates Do Not Fix Learning: Influence Profile and Budgeted Auditing in Iterative Self-Training}%
\aistatsauthor{Anonymous Authors}%
\aistatsaddress{}%
]
\begin{abstract}
True- and false-acceptance rates summarize verifier quality but do not
determine self-training outcomes. In a stopped-gradient logistic model,
two acceptance rules have identical rates, yield, and precision at a common
initialization yet induce opposite updates. Under resampled population
gradient flow, their rates remain matched while one learner improves and
the other converges below its initial accuracy. The separation comes from
concentrating accepted errors on high-influence examples. For a specified
group-targeted correctness audit, we prove the sharp uniform threshold
$q\geq1-a$ for monotone population improvement, where $a$ is the
correct-acceptance probability. Finite-pool identities and smooth-loss bounds
connect this analysis to measured parameter changes while distinguishing
reference loss from task risk. We develop controlled graph and arithmetic
comparisons with shared initialization and pre-audit matching, alongside
finite-budget auditing policies outside the population guarantee. In
five-block, four-round graph studies with Qwen3-1.7B/4B and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, both
selection arms improve in mean.
Qwen3-1.7B's unaudited arms nevertheless differ in nonshortest-error incidence.
Separate one-step graph studies with Qwen3-1.7B/4B and Llama-3.2-3B yield
small primary ID accuracy contrasts.
\end{abstract}
