\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

Let $x\sim\mathcal D$, $y\sim p_{\theta_0}(\cdot\mid x)$,
$c(x,y)\in\{0,1\}$ be correctness, and $A_v\in\{0,1\}$ be rule
$v\in\{R,S\}$'s possibly random acceptance. Sampling accuracy is
$P(\theta)=\Pr_{x\sim\mathcal D,\,y\sim p_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}(c=1)$.
With $P_0=P(\theta_0)\in(0,1)$, define
\begin{align}
 t_v&=\Pr(A_v=1\mid c=1),&
 f_v&=\Pr(A_v=1\mid c=0),\nonumber\\
 Z_v&=t_vP_0+f_v(1-P_0),&
 \pi_v&=\frac{t_vP_0}{Z_v},\label{eq:rates}
\end{align}
Here yield $Z_v=\Pr(A_v=1)$ is assumed positive, and $\pi_v$ is
accepted-data precision. Matching $(t_v,f_v)$ on the common distribution
also fixes $(Z_v,\pi_v)$.

For loss $\ell$, training on accepted responses defines
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_v(\vartheta;\theta_0)
 &=\frac{\mathbb E_{x,y\sim p_{\theta_0}}[A_v\ell(\vartheta;x,y)]}{Z_v},\\
 G_v&=\left.\nabla_\vartheta\mathcal L_v\right|_{\vartheta=\theta_0}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:stopped-objective}
\end{equation}
The \emph{stopped gradient} $G_v$ holds the sampling law, acceptance, and
$Z_v$ fixed under $\nabla_\vartheta$. The following identity isolates
within-class selection moments; Section~\ref{sec:logistic} shows that
matched rates need not fix them.

\begin{lemma}[Matched rates leave a conditional influence gap]
\label{lem:influence}
Let $g=\nabla\ell(\theta_0;x,y)$ be integrable and differentiation
in~\eqref{eq:stopped-objective} commute with expectation. Couple $A_S,A_R$
over common $(x,y)$, preserving each rule's conditional law.
If both rules have the same $(t,f)$ and $Z>0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 G_S-G_R={}&\frac{P_0}{Z}\operatorname{Cov}(A_S-A_R,g\mid c=1)\\
 &+\frac{1-P_0}{Z}\operatorname{Cov}(A_S-A_R,g\mid c=0).
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:influence-gap}
\end{equation}
Covariance is componentwise, and the identity is independent of the coupling.
\end{lemma}
The gradients agree exactly when the weighted covariances cancel;
Section~\ref{sec:logistic} constructs a nonzero gap. The proof and sufficient
regularity conditions are in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

The population results concern $P$. Experiments instead measure task risk
under fixed greedy decoding $q_{\mathrm{dec},\theta}$ and an ID or OOD
prompt distribution $\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}$:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\mathrm{task}}(\theta)=
 \mathbb E_{\substack{x\sim\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}\\
 y\sim q_{\mathrm{dec},\theta}(\cdot\mid x)}}[1-c(x,y)].
 \label{eq:task-risk}
\end{equation}
It equals $1-P$ when $q_{\mathrm{dec},\theta}=p_\theta$ and
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}=\mathcal D$; fixed decoding settings do not
freeze the output distribution.
On $n_{\mathrm{ref}}$ disjoint canonical correct responses, reference loss is
$\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta)=n_{\mathrm{ref}}^{-1}
\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\mathrm{ref}}}\ell_i(\theta)$, with per-response token-mean
losses from~\eqref{eq:response-loss}.
For $h=\nabla\mathcal R_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta_0)$ and actual update
$\Delta\theta$, $h^T\Delta\theta$ diagnoses first-order surrogate change,
not task-risk improvement.
