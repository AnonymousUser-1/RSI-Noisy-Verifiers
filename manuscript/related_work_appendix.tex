\section{Extended Related Work}
\label{app:extended-related-work}

This section continues Section~\ref{sec:related} with comparisons to budgeted
cleaning, recursive synthetic-data degradation, evaluator supervision,
reward learning, and exploration-policy improvement.

\paragraph{Budgeted data cleaning.}
ActiveClean prioritizes records for progressive cleaning and gives
convergence guarantees for convex-loss models \citep{krishnan2016activeclean}.
Both settings allocate limited cleaning effort with the learner in mind.
Our intervention instead queries correctness of selected generated responses and
removes audited errors, with a sharp uniform threshold only for the stated
population policy. The finite-budget language-model policies remain
empirical interventions, not extensions of ActiveClean's guarantee.

\paragraph{Recursive synthetic-data degradation.}
\citet{shumailov2024collapse} analyze and demonstrate distributional
degradation from recursive training on generated data. Our separation
instead arises in a resampled population model without finite-sample
tail loss, and compares acceptance rules at matched rates. A harmful
one-step update is not itself evidence of multi-generation model collapse.

\paragraph{Outcome and process verification.}
\citet{cobbe2021verifiers} train verifiers to assess generated mathematical
solutions and use their scores for candidate selection at inference time.
\citet{lightman2023verify} compare supervision of final outcomes with
supervision of individual reasoning steps. These distinctions concern the
information used to train and apply an evaluator. Our intervention concerns
the identities of accepted errors at matched operating statistics. We use
controlled acceptance rules and explicit correctness labels; the experiment
does not itself constitute a comparison of learned verifier-training methods.

\paragraph{Reward models, critics, and self-rewarding.}
InstructGPT learns a reward model from preference comparisons and optimizes a
policy with PPO, using a value function initialized from the reward model
\citep{ouyang2022instructgpt}. A reward model supplies the optimization signal,
whereas the critic estimates values used by the policy optimizer. Our
acceptance rule instead selects targets for supervised training. The
counterexample is derived for that stopped-gradient objective and is not a
theorem about PPO or actor--critic convergence; its object is the supervised
update induced by an accepted training subset.
Self-Rewarding Language Models instead obtain feedback from the model
itself and use iterative DPO \citep{yuan2024selfrewarding}; this is not
correctness-filtered supervised self-training.

\paragraph{Recursive improvement of exploration policies.}
Dream-RSI \citep{zheng2026dreamrsi} studies recursive improvement of exploration
policies by replaying recorded discovery trees to evaluate changes in
branching, parallel scheduling, and stopping. The discovery models,
evaluator, and execution interface remain fixed; policy code changes.
Its selection guarantee concerns the fixed historical replay objective,
not monotonic improvement on new online discoveries or an independent
task distribution. Our complementary question concerns parameter updates
under matched selection statistics. Common candidate pools and records
support reproducibility, but independent task risk remains the outcome;
we introduce no adaptive exploration controller into the intervention.
