\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Aggregate acceptance statistics leave gradient structure undetermined;
they do not make verifier accuracy irrelevant or every systematic error
harmful. Both four-round graph arms improve in each model, and the random arm contains many target
errors. These runs do not exhibit the counterexample's opposite learning
directions. Task signatures identify error
types, not measured influence; arithmetic lacks exact-length control.

The population guarantee assumes known groups and fresh resampling.
Its vanishing-query-fraction construction trades lower group mass for
larger magnitude contrast; it does not guarantee universally cheap auditing.
Empirical auditing changes weights and optimizer steps, while later
arm-specific pools no longer share the initial matching guarantees.
Query counts exclude selection and evaluation labels. Four rounds and five
blocks do not establish sustained recursive improvement or broad predictive
validity. Reference projections remain surrogate diagnostics without a
certified smoothness bound. Incomplete raw-response coverage and cost ledgers
limit broader independent rejudging and efficiency claims.
